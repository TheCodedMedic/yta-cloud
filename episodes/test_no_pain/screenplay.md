# Episode 05: What If You Couldn't Feel Pain?

**Speakers:**
- N: narrator (What_If_In_Theory_Narrator)
- YOU: the protagonist (WhatIf_Protagonist)
- THEO: your best friend, same age (WhatIf_Friend_Theo)
- MIA: your sister, 12 (WhatIf_Young_Girl_Bright)
- GRANDAD: your grandad, a nurse for forty years (WhatIf_Night_Guard)
- DOCTOR: the surgeon (WhatIf_Adult_Man_Deadpan)

Each line below is one beat (one voice clip). Lines in [brackets] are picture and sound notes, not spoken.

Sources: Cox et al., *Nature* 2006 (SCN9A, the Pakistani families, the street performer who died at 14 after jumping from a roof); Habib et al., *British Journal of Anaesthesia* 2019 (Jo Cameron, FAAH-OUT); the spinal withdrawal reflex (about 100 ms, faster than conscious perception); Paul Brand's work on leprosy and pain.

## 1. Cold open
[Kitchen, Saturday morning. You lift a frying pan by its metal handle straight off the flame. Steam. Your face does nothing. Mia stops chewing.]
N: It starts with a frying pan. Saturday, nine in the morning. You pick it up by the handle.
N: The metal handle. Straight off the flame.
N: Your sister stops chewing. You look down at your hand. The skin is going red. And you feel nothing.
MIA: Why aren't you screaming?
N: That's the question, isn't it. So, what if you couldn't feel pain?
[Title: WHAT IF YOU COULDN'T FEEL PAIN?]
N: Not less pain. None. No stings, no aches, no headaches, no hot handle. Ever again.
N: It sounds like a superpower. For about a week, it is.

## 2. The rules
N: Here's how it works. Rule one. Nothing hurts. Not a paper cut, not a broken arm, not a dentist's drill.
N: Rule two. You still get hurt. The burn still burns. The bone still breaks. You just don't get the message.
N: Rule three. Nobody can tell. You look fine. You always look fine. Remember that one.
N: And here's the strange part. This is real. There are people alive right now who were born this way.
N: In 2006, doctors in Cambridge studied families in Pakistan with a broken gene called SCN9A. One tiny switch inside the nerves, stuck off. None of them had ever felt pain.
N: One boy earned money in the street, walking on hot coals and pushing knives through his arms. He never felt a thing.
N: He died at fourteen. He jumped off a roof, because nothing had ever told him not to.

## 3. Discovery and tests
N: But you don't know any of that yet. You know you just held a hot pan for six seconds, and your Saturday has suddenly got very interesting.
N: So you test it. Like anyone would.
N: Test one. The hottest sauce in the shop. The one with the skull on the label. Theo cries from across the table.
THEO: Your face isn't even red. That's not normal, bro.
YOU: Tastes like ketchup.
N: Test two. The dentist. The one you've been avoiding for three years.
N: No injection. No gas. You watch the drill go in and think about lunch.
N: Test three. Five-a-side. The tackle that would have ended your season. You get up, pick the ball out of the net, and walk it back to the centre circle.
THEO: Bro. Your ankle is the wrong shape.
YOU: Feels fine.
N: And it does. That's the problem.
N: Plus. Headaches are gone. The gym stops being a punishment. You do a hundred press-ups, because nothing tells you to stop at thirty.
N: Minus. On Tuesday you bite your tongue at lunch, and you don't find out until Theo points at the blood.
N: Plus. You become a legend. The guy who can't feel. There's a video. It has four hundred thousand views.
N: Minus. The blister on your heel turns into a hole in your heel, because you walked on it for a week.
N: Here's the part the movies skip. Your body was never built for this. Pain is the only reason you ever moved your hand off a hot thing.
N: Touch a stove, and your spine yanks your arm back before your brain even knows. About a tenth of a second. It's a reflex, and it runs on the same alarm.
N: You just ripped the alarm out of the wall.

## 4. The science and the warning
N: There's a woman in Scotland called Jo Cameron. Doctors found her in 2019. A different broken gene, the same result. She has never felt pain either.
N: She burns her arm on the oven and only notices when she smells it. So she checks her hands every night, the way you'd check a car for scratches.
N: Because when you can't feel the damage, you have to go looking for it.
N: And you? You stop looking. Why would you? You feel great.
N: Your grandad was a nurse for forty years. He watches you limp into the kitchen like nothing is wrong.
GRANDAD: Pain's not your enemy, son. Pain's the only friend who never lies to you.
YOU: Then I've got one less liar to worry about.
N: You'll remember that line. Just not yet.

## 5. The trap
N: Because here's what you build. A life with no warnings.
N: You skip the doctor. You sleep on your arm and wake up with a hand that doesn't work until noon. You eat food that's too hot, and your mouth becomes a map of burns you never felt.
N: Theo starts bringing a first-aid kit to football. Not for the team. For you.
N: And one Friday, around dinner, something in your stomach goes wrong. You don't know. You can't know.
N: You just notice you're not hungry. Then you notice you're cold. Then you notice nothing, because noticing was never your job. It was pain's job.
N: And here's where rule three comes back. Nobody can tell. You look fine.

## 6. The climax
N: Except one person. Theo. He's known you for eleven years. He looks at you across the table, and he doesn't see fine.
THEO: You've gone grey. You're sweating, and it's cold in here. When did you last eat?
YOU: I'm fine. Nothing hurts.
THEO: Yeah. That's what scares me.
N: Your appendix burst two hours ago. For anyone else, that's the worst pain of their life. For you, it's a Friday.
N: Theo drives. You tell him he's overreacting the whole way. At the hospital, a doctor with grey hair looks at the scan and goes very quiet.
DOCTOR: Most people walk in here screaming. You walked in because somebody looked at you. That somebody just saved your life.
N: Surgery takes an hour. Recovery takes three weeks. You feel none of it.
N: But you feel this. Lying there, you finally understand what your grandad meant.
N: Pain was never the enemy. Pain was the alarm. And when you can't hear the alarm, you need somebody watching for the smoke.

## 7. The ending
N: So you change. Not the power. You.
N: You check your hands every night, like Jo. You see a doctor every month, whether you like it or not. You let Theo look at you, and when he says you've gone grey, you believe him.
N: And at dinner, your sister pokes the scar on your stomach.
MIA: Did that hurt?
YOU: No.
MIA: Good. Then this won't.
N: And she punches your arm, as hard as a twelve-year-old can.
N: It doesn't hurt. But you laugh until it should.
N: Then your phone buzzes. Two in the morning. Theo.
N: "Mate. I just put my hand on the hob. And I didn't feel anything."
[Hold: the frying pan on the dark hob, the handle glowing. Hard cut to black.]
