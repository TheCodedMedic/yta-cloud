# Episode 04: What If You Could Go Back in Time?

**Speakers:**
- N: narrator
- YOU: the protagonist
- JAMIE: your younger brother, 17, voiced with the friend voice
- COACH: older, voiced with the guard/old-man voice

Each line below is one beat (one voice clip). Lines in [brackets] are picture and sound notes, not spoken.

## 1. Cold open
[Night street, rain. You sprint. Rain stops in mid-air; the camera orbits. Lightning crawls off you. The street smears into streaks.]
N: It's raining. You're running. And then the rain stops.
N: Not stops falling. Stops. Every drop hangs in the air like broken glass.
N: Lightning crawls off your shoulders. The street stretches into lines of light. And the world tears open.
N: Tonight, you're going to break time. You don't care. You only want to save one person.
[One-second flash: a city rewriting itself. Hard cut. Title: WHAT IF YOU COULD GO BACK IN TIME?]
N: So, what if you could go back in time? Not in a machine. On foot. By running faster than time itself.
N: It sounds like the best power anyone has ever had. It is. Right up until the world starts to change.

## 2. The rules
N: Here's how it works. Rule one. The faster you run, the further back you go. A jog buys you a second. A sprint, a minute. Flat out, you could lose a year.
N: Rule two. You arrive where you were, as you were. Same clothes. Same street. You can't go back to a place you've never been.
N: Rule three. Every jump leaves a scar. A crack of light in the sky that only you can see. One scar is nothing. Wait until you see a hundred.
N: Here's the strange part. Some of this is real. Time really does run at different speeds in different places.
N: The clocks on GPS satellites run about thirty-eight millionths of a second a day faster than clocks on the ground. Engineers correct for it every single day. If they didn't, the map on your phone would drift by kilometres.
N: Going forward in time is just physics. Going back is where physics starts arguing with itself.

## 3. Discovery and tests
N: It starts on a Tuesday. You're late. Of course you're late.
N: The bus doors are closing. You run. And something in your legs keeps going long after your body should have stopped.
N: The world slows down. A pigeon hangs mid-flap. A man's coffee floats out of his cup in a perfect brown ribbon.
N: Then, a flash. And you're standing at the corner again. Ten seconds ago.
N: You watch the same pigeon take off. The same man spill the same coffee. This time, you catch the cup.
YOU: Okay. Okay, okay, okay.
N: So you test it. Like anyone would.
N: The bus. Missed it. Run. Caught it.
N: The maths test. Question seven. Wrong. Run. Right.
N: Thursday, five-a-side. The keeper dives left. Run. You shoot right.
COACH: How did you know where he'd go?
YOU: Lucky guess.
N: Three tests. Three wins. And nobody notices you're breathing like you just finished a marathon.
N: Because here's the part the movies skip. Moving that fast burns energy like a furnace. You eat four breakfasts. You sleep eleven hours. You are hungry all the time.
N: You think about the lottery, for about a second. Then you think about the only thing you've wanted for a year.

## 4. The want
N: Twelve months ago, on the fourteenth of March, your little brother Jamie crossed Mill Road on his way to meet you.
N: The light was green for him. The van didn't stop.
N: You were late that night too.
N: You still have his last voice note. Eleven seconds. You know every word.
JAMIE: Where are you? I'm at the crossing. Hurry up, the film starts at eight. I'm getting the big popcorn, so you owe me.
N: Ten seconds is a cup of coffee. A year is thirty-one million seconds.
N: To go back that far, you'd have to run faster than you have ever run. Faster than anything has ever run.
YOU: I don't care. I'm going.
N: So that night, you go to the long, straight road by the river. And you run.

## 5. The jump
[Time tunnel. Sound drains away, colour drains away, then the year rewinds around you.]
N: First the sound goes. Then the colour. The streetlights stretch into threads. The river flows backwards.
N: Days peel away around you like pages. Snow falls up into the clouds. Leaves fly back onto the trees. People walk backwards out of shops, putting their shopping back.
N: Winter. Autumn. Summer. Spring. A whole year rewinds in a single breath.
N: And then you're there. The fourteenth of March. Seven fifty-eight p.m. Mill Road.

## 6. The save
N: There he is. Hood up. Earphones in. Looking at his phone instead of the road.
N: The van comes round the corner. Too fast. Again.
N: You don't think. You run.
N: Time stretches like warm glass. The headlights crawl. Jamie's foot comes down on the crossing, very, very slowly.
N: You reach him with half a second to spare. And you pull.
[Full silence. The van passes in slow motion, inches away. The music is gone.]
JAMIE: Whoa! Where did you come from?
N: The van is gone. Your brother is standing on the pavement. Annoyed. Confused. Alive.
JAMIE: You're actually early. That's a first.
YOU: Yeah. Let's just go and see the film.
N: And you laugh so hard you have to sit down on the kerb.

## 7. The butterfly effect
N: You run home. Forward this time. Back to now.
N: And for one perfect morning, everything is right. Jamie is at the kitchen table, eating your cereal.
JAMIE: Why are you staring at me like that? It's weird.
N: Then you step outside.
N: The café on the corner, the one you've walked into every morning for three years, is a car park.
N: Your best friend walks straight past you. He doesn't know your name. You never met him, because you met him on the night of the accident. And now, that night never happened.
N: This is the butterfly effect. And it isn't just a saying.
N: In 1961, a scientist called Edward Lorenz reran a weather simulation. To save time, he typed in a number with three decimal places instead of six. A difference smaller than one part in a thousand.
N: Weeks later, inside the simulation, the weather was completely different.
N: A tiny change. A totally different world. That's chaos theory. And you didn't change a decimal. You changed a whole life.
N: So you try to fix it. Of course you do.
N: You run back a week to make sure you meet your friend again. You do. But when you come home, the tower by the river, the tallest building in the city, was never built.
N: You run back again to fix that. When you return, half the faces on your street are strangers. Your mum's photo wall shows a holiday you never went on.
N: Every fix makes two new problems. Every jump leaves another scar. And the sky is starting to look like a cracked windscreen.
N: Physicists genuinely argue about this. If you change the past, which present do you come back to?
N: One idea, from a Russian physicist named Igor Novikov, says the universe simply won't let a contradiction happen. Another says every change splits off a brand new timeline. Nobody knows which is true. You're finding out the hard way.
N: The cracks spread. Clocks start to skip. A bird freezes in mid-air for a second, then carries on. People stop mid-sentence, and don't notice.
JAMIE: Hey. Why does today feel like it already happened?
N: He shouldn't remember. Nobody should remember.

## 8. The last jump
N: And now you understand the deal. You can have your brother. Or you can have the world you broke to get him back.
N: You stand at the end of the long road by the river. One more run. The biggest of your life.
YOU: I'm sorry. Whatever happens next, I'm sorry.
N: You run. Lightning. Streaks. Silence.
N: And we don't get to see what you chose.

## 9. The ending
N: Morning. Birds. The smell of toast.
N: You open your eyes. The café is back on the corner. The tower is back by the river. The sky is clean.
N: And from the kitchen, a voice.
JAMIE: Oi! You finished the cereal again!
N: He's here. The world's here. You did it. You actually did it.
N: Then your phone buzzes. One new message. From your own number.
N: Sent tomorrow.
N: "Don't go back again. Whatever you do."
N: You didn't write that. Not yet.
[Every clock in the street stops on the same second. A crack of light runs across the morning sky. Camera cranes up through the window: the whole city frozen. Hard cut to black.]

YOU: I already did.
