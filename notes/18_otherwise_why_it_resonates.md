# 18 · OTHERWISE, all three videos: why one hit 78k and the others didn't (2026-10-23)

Studied from 480p downloads, captions, frame sheets every 8 s, and a voice/music split (Demucs). Downloads are in `source/otherwise/`; delete them after the study. We learn from it; we don't copy it.

All three share the same template, voice, art style and length. So the difference is the **subject**, not the craft.

## Video 1: Predict the Market (754 views)
- **Hook:** "Your phone shows you tomorrow's chart." It's clever, but it only lands if you trade.
- **Emotional core:** the dad, 30 years of bus fare in a jar, aching knees ("Rain's coming." "My knees do."). The best moment is a quiet one: "I lost everything, Dad." "Well, slow money is still money."
- **Why it stayed small:**
  - **The stakes are money.** Most viewers have never felt a liquidation, so the fear is borrowed.
  - **Jargon** (drawdown, 100x, liquidation) needs explaining, which costs the emotion time.
  - **The hero is a winner** for most of the video, and it's hard to feel for someone who can't lose.
- **What works anyway:**
  - the line "You were right about the price. You were wrong about yourself.";
  - the dad's planted line paying off;
  - Rocky's running gag.

## Video 2: Rewind 10 Seconds (1.5k views)
- **Hook:** the wet stair, then cereal → ceiling → floor. A small, physical, funny moment: brilliant.
- **The middle is a power fantasy:** basketball ×40, coin tosses, six first dates, job interviews. Fun, but it's wish fulfilment. You enjoy it; you don't feel it.
- **Emotional core:** the thrill addiction ("The first jump felt like flying. The 10th felt like stairs.") and the dad: "Accidents happen to people in a hurry."
- **The best sequence in all three videos:** the 450 m fall. 37 attempts, the rule-two trap springs, and the dad's line saves you on attempt 31. A climax solved by character.
- **Why it stayed small:** sensation-seeking isn't most people's problem. The lesson ("the dangerous decisions are in the hour before") is wise but abstract.

## Video 3: How Many Times You'll See Someone (78k views)
- **The hero object is a feeling made visible:** a number over every head. Everyone instantly asks "what's mine for my mum? my best friend?" The viewer runs the premise on their own life within 30 seconds.
- **Every scene is something the viewer has lived:**
  - a parent on the phone ("You eating vegetables? When are you coming home?" "Soon. Next time.");
  - the college roommate who swore "every weekend, forever" (he's a 4);
  - "We have to get dinner sometime." "You will not get dinner sometime.";
  - the stranger at the bus stop you've never spoken to in 8 months;
  - the best friend moving away.
- **The science hurts because it's about you:**
  - Dunbar's 150;
  - Hall: about 50, 90 and 200 hours to become friends;
  - Mollenhorst: half your circle is gone in 7 years, "not because of a fight, nobody slammed a door";
  - time with friends falls to about 45 minutes a day by 30.

  Every number turns into a quiet "oh no, that's me."
- **The flaw is human:** the power didn't make him braver; it made him **hide**, avoiding goodbyes. The cost is self-inflicted. He shrank Rocky's number by skipping the party ("The party happened. You just weren't in it.").
- **The tiny specific memory:** at eight, a kid pushed you off the slide, and Rocky shoved him back and gave you half his sandwich. "That was the whole interview." One concrete childhood detail does more than any VFX.
- **The twist gives the viewer agency:** the numbers were "a forecast, like the weather". Change the calendar and the forecast changes: 41 visits, plane tickets. Hope you can act on tonight.
- **The call to action lives outside the video:** "maybe text someone today before they become a one." People did, and commented about it. Comments are part of why it spread.
- **The visuals are humble:**
  - flat 2D, matte, mostly daylight;
  - many ordinary places (tube, coffee counter, bar, office, wedding, bus stop, airport, ferry, park);
  - one costume colour per person, black heads with a hair curl, eyebrows doing all the acting;
  - one big face close-up with a tear under an umbrella.

  Nothing flashy. The story carries it.

## The rules this gives us
1. **The power must be a metaphor for something every viewer has felt** (regret, guilt, losing people, running out of time). Money and thrills lose; love, grief and regret win.
2. **Within 30 seconds, make the viewer run the premise on their own life.**
3. **Scenes come from everyday life:** phone calls with parents, siblings stealing food, being late, the voice note you replay.
4. **The science lands on "you" in the next sentence, and it should hurt a little.**
5. **The hero's flaw causes the cost.** The power doesn't fail; the person does.
6. **One tiny, specific memory beats a montage.**
7. **The climax is solved by character:** a planted line or habit comes back.
8. **End with something the viewer can do today, in real life**, so they comment about having done it.
9. **Daylight and ordinary places.** Night and VFX only where the story peaks.
10. **Acting is in the eyes, brows and body**, not the mouth. People talking are shown reacting, listening and moving, not just flapping.

## The music (measured from the Demucs voice/music split)
- **Continuous.** Music runs under the whole video. True silence happens only 1–2 times per video, on the hardest drop:
  - video 1 at 5:26, just after "you were wrong about yourself";
  - video 2 at 4:52 (rule four fires) and 6:14 (the foot slips);
  - video 3 has none at all.
- **A new cue roughly every 30–90 s, cut exactly on story turns.** In video 3:
  - 0:12, after the hook;
  - 1:06, the Dunbar science;
  - 2:50, the friendship-hours data;
  - 3:38, "one more time, then never again";
  - 4:00, the avoidance chapter;
  - 6:30, the bus-stop payoff;
  - 6:58, the happy landing;
  - 7:38, the mystery hook.
- **Tempo** sits mostly at 100–120 BPM; the cues change key and colour, not energy.
- **Swells:** louder only on the action peak. In video 2 the tower climb rises about 8 dB for 20 s, then drops into the fall.
- **Level** (RMS, music against voice):
  - about 9–13 dB under the voice while someone speaks;
  - rising to about 5–7 dB under in the pauses.

  That is **much louder than our Episode 04 mix** (about 20+ dB under). Their music is a real presence that swells in every gap.
- **For us:**
  - mix the beds at about −12 dB RMS under the voice, with ducking, and let them swell in the gaps;
  - change the cue on every story turn;
  - silence only on the 1–2 hardest moments.
