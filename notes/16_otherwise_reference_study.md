# 16 · Reference study: OTHERWISE, "What If You Could See How Many Times You'll See Someone Again?" (2026-10-23)

**The video:** https://youtu.be/COFKHLLRjPQ
- Channel: OTHERWISE, 280 subscribers, their 3rd video.
- 62k views in about a day; 8:04 long.
- The description credits no music.
- Studied from a 480p download, which was deleted after the study (notes and numbers only).

## Why it works (from frames and captions)
- **A visual premise you can read at a glance:** a number floating over every head. The number is the hero object, and it changes on story beats.
- **Research made visible:**
  - real studies get on-screen source chips: Robin Dunbar (Oxford), Jeffrey Hall (2018), Gerald Mollenhorst (Utrecht, 2009);
  - clean charts: hours-together curves, Dunbar's circles, dot grids for "switched jobs".
- **Time chips:**
  - "WEEK 1 · MON", "WEEK 5 · FRI", "YEAR 7", "NOVEMBER";
  - a calendar flip, a checklist (Tonight / Going-away party / Airport).
- **Strike-through words as emotional turns:** ~~FATE~~, ~~NEXT TIME~~.
- **Sets:**
  - simple flat 2D: tube carriage, bus stop, office, bar, airport, ferry, bedroom at night;
  - each with one strong colour block and plenty of air;
  - a calm, minimal cast (black-head stick figures, one costume colour each).
- **Ending:** an emotional payoff (the bus stop friendship), then the novel page "Tell them…".

## The music (measured)
- **Continuous** under the whole narration. No silences, no big hits or risers.
- **Tempo** about 100–105 BPM: gentle and steady, not cinematic.
- **Harmony** around A minor, C and F: warm and slightly bittersweet.
- **Mixed low:** the master is about −18.4 LUFS with a narrow loudness range (LRA 5.1). The voice sits well on top, the bass is warm, and the high end is soft.
- **Reads as:** soft lo-fi / warm indie-electronic, or gentle piano with a light beat. It is "calm storytelling", not trailer music.

## Takeaways for us
1. **Under narration:** one warm, calm, steady music bed (about 100 BPM, soft beat, piano or keys) for most of the film. Save the cinematic hits and silences for 2–3 peak moments only.
2. **More research on screen:** named studies with year and place as small chips.
3. **More time chips and strike-through turns;** fewer giant slams.
4. **One clear hero object** that carries the premise through the whole video.
