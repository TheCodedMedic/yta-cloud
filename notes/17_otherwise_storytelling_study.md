## The template they repeat (every video)
1. **Cold open inside the power, mid-moment** (first line, 5–10 s): "It starts on a Monday…", "You're halfway down the stairs when your sock finds the one wet step", "Your phone shows you tomorrow's chart." A tiny, physical, relatable moment, then a jolt.
2. **The title question at about 20–40 s**, after you're already hooked: "What if you could…?"
3. **The rules, numbered and plain:** "Rule one… Rule two, *remember rule two, it matters later*… There's one more rule. You don't know it yet." This plants the climax in the rules.
4. **One real study per act, named:** Robin Dunbar (Oxford, 150 people), Jeffrey Hall (2018, 50/90/200 hours), Gerald Mollenhorst (Utrecht, half your network gone in 7 years), Brazil day traders (97 % lose), the 50 ms error signal, the $300M cable for 3 ms. The number is always turned into *your* life immediately.
5. **The fun tests in a rhythm of three** (high five, long goodbye, the dog; 40 shots, coin toss, rock-paper-scissors; first date ×6). Short lines, quick jokes, "Bro…".
6. **The Plus/Minus scorecard:** "Plus, … Minus, …". It alternates delight and cost, and the minuses grow until one becomes the plot.
7. **The quiet sting:** a small human cost that lands harder than spectacle ("Hello and goodbye at the exact same time"; "2 whole days nobody saw"; "Winning stops feeling like anything").
8. **The trap the hero builds himself:** hiding from goodbyes; chasing thrills; betting 100×. The rule planted earlier springs it (rule two: 10 seconds back is not enough for a 12-second fall).
9. **The climax solved by character, not power:** the dad's line comes back ("Accidents happen to people in a hurry"; "Slow money is still money"). The lesson is said once, simply: "The dangerous decisions are never in the last 10 seconds. They're in the hour before."
10. **A warm landing:** the bus-stop girl, "Want to miss the bus with me?", "Bro, what are we buying? Groceries?"
11. **The mystery hook last:** handwriting on the screen, a dotted line writing itself, "whoever is on the other end of that line is about to call back." Then a comment question plus subscribe, said inside the story's voice.

## Craft details
- **Second person throughout** ("you"); the narrator is wry and calm, never shouty. Lines are short, about 1,200 words for 8 minutes (about 150 wpm with breathing room).
- **A recurring small cast across episodes** as a soft universe: Rocky ("Bro, what are we buying?"), the bus-driver dad with one wise line, the girl at the bus stop with the bad book. Running gags pay off across videos.
- **Callbacks inside the episode:** the bad book; "next time"; "crab"; "heart rate 62". The first scene is repeated at the end, changed.
- **Numbers as emotion:** a 3 over your best friend's head; 41 visits; 37 tries; 6 minutes of falling.
- **The premise is a metaphor** for a real human problem (drifting friendships, impatience, greed). That is why people relate: the power is just the lens.
- **The music** (study 16): one calm, warm, continuous bed around 100 BPM, low under the voice; no trailer hits.

## What this means for our writing (without copying)
- Open inside a small physical moment, then ask the question.
- Number the rules, and plant the one that will break.
- Make every science fact land on the viewer's own life in the next sentence.
- Use a plus/minus rhythm for the middle.
- Solve the climax with a character trait or a line set up earlier.
- End warm, then hook with a mystery.
